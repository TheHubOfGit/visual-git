# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who work in a Git repository without understanding Git: students, designers, analysts, writers, and new developers. They open Visual Git when they are unsure what state their project is in, often a little anxious about breaking something.

## Product Purpose

Show a local repository's history as a map and say in plain words where the person is, what has changed, and what they can safely do next. Success is a newcomer answering "what branch am I on, what have I changed, and how do I save it" without searching the web.

## Brand Personality

Clear, calm, literal. It reads like good public signage: it tells you where you are and what each thing is, and never shows off.

## Anti-references

The generic developer-tool look: dark navy with a blue accent, pills and floating cards, glowing dots, tiny uppercase tracked labels. Also avoid Git GUIs that assume the vocabulary (ahead/behind, HEAD, staging area) without explaining it.

## Design Principles

1. Plain words first, Git's term beside them, so people learn the real vocabulary as they go.
2. Always answer "where am I and is my work saved" before anything else.
3. Looking never changes anything; every action that does says what will happen and which Git command it stands for, and waits for a yes.
4. One structure, three looks (Transit map, Logbook, Blueprint). A theme changes drawing style and type, never where things are.
5. No destructive or networked operations: nothing that deletes, rewrites history, or uploads.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast in all three themes, full keyboard operation, visible focus, state never carried by colour alone (labels accompany every colour), and reduced-motion support.
